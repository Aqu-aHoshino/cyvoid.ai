THREAT INTELLIGENCE

Understand Cybersecurity Threats

Explore common threats, understand how they work, learn warning signs, and follow practical steps to reduce risk.

Social Engineering

1. Phishing

Phishing is a deceptive attempt to make people reveal passwords, financial details, or other sensitive information. Attackers may pretend to represent a trusted company, school, service, or person through email, text messages, or websites.

Warning signs: Urgent demands, unexpected attachments, suspicious links, and requests for login details.

How to avoid it: Verify the sender independently, inspect website addresses, avoid unexpected links, and never share verification codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Malicious Software

2. Malware

Malware is software designed to perform harmful, unwanted actions on a device. It may steal information, disrupt normal operations, spy on activity, or install other unwanted programs.

Warning signs: Unexpected pop-ups, unfamiliar apps, unusual device behavior, or security alerts.

How to avoid it: Download apps from trusted sources, keep software updated, review app permissions, and use reputable security tools.

Data Extortion

3. Ransomware

Ransomware is a type of malware that can prevent access to files or systems, often by encrypting data. Attackers may demand payment to claim they will restore access or avoid releasing stolen information.

Warning signs: Unexpected file access problems, ransom messages, or suspicious changes to files.

How to avoid it: Maintain offline or otherwise protected backups, install updates, avoid suspicious attachments, and limit unnecessary account permissions.

Account Security

4. Password Attacks

Password attacks involve attempts to gain access to accounts through stolen credentials, reused passwords, guessing, or deceptive login pages. A compromised password can put multiple accounts at risk when it is reused.

Warning signs: Unexpected login notifications, password-reset messages you did not request, or unfamiliar account sessions.

How to avoid it: Use a unique, long password for every account, enable multifactor authentication, and use a reputable password manager if appropriate.

Human Manipulation

5. Social Engineering

Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information or performing actions that may compromise their security. An attacker may pretend to be a friend, authority figure, technician, or trusted organization.

Warning signs: Pressure to act immediately, secrecy demands, unusual requests, or attempts to bypass normal procedures.

How to avoid it: Pause before acting, verify requests through a separate trusted channel, and never disclose passwords or one-time security codes.

Network Security

6. Unsafe Network Connections

Untrusted or poorly secured networks can expose users to privacy and security risks. Fake Wi-Fi hotspots may imitate legitimate public networks to trick people into connecting.

Warning signs: Unexpected hotspot names, suspicious connection pages, or requests to install unfamiliar certificates or apps.

How to avoid it: Confirm the official network name, avoid sensitive activity on untrusted networks, use HTTPS websites, and turn off automatic connection to unknown Wi-Fi.

Privacy

7. Data Breaches

A data breach occurs when protected or private information is accessed or disclosed without authorization. Information may be exposed through compromised accounts, insecure systems, or mistakes.

Warning signs: Official breach notifications, unfamiliar account activity, or evidence that information has been exposed.

How to avoid it: Use unique passwords, enable multifactor authentication, share less personal information publicly, and monitor important accounts for suspicious activity.

Online Scams

8. Fake Websites and Online Scams

Fraudulent websites and online offers may imitate legitimate services to steal information or money. Some advertise prizes, discounts, jobs, or services that are not genuine.

Warning signs: Unrealistic offers, inconsistent website addresses, unusual payment requests, or missing contact information.

How to avoid it: Visit official websites directly, research unfamiliar sellers, verify offers through trusted sources, and avoid sending money or personal details under pressure.

Privacy and Monitoring

9. Spyware

Spyware is software that monitors or collects information about a device or its user without appropriate knowledge or permission. Depending on its behavior, it may collect browsing data, credentials, or other private information.

Warning signs: Unfamiliar applications, unusual permissions, or unexpected security warnings.

How to avoid it: Review installed apps and permissions, install updates, use trusted download sources, and remove suspicious software with help from a reputable security tool.

Good security habit: If you suspect an account has been compromised, use a trusted device to change its password, revoke unfamiliar sessions, enable multifactor authentication, and contact the service's official support if necessary.
SECURITY RESOURCES

Free Security Apps and Tools

Explore reputable security resources. Availability, free features, and supported devices may vary. Always verify that you are visiting the official website.

Microsoft Defender

Windows includes Microsoft Defender Antivirus, which provides built-in malware protection. Other Defender products and features may have different requirements.

Official website

Google Password Manager

Helps save and manage passwords associated with your Google account and can help identify certain compromised or weak passwords.

Open Password Manager

Google Safe Browsing

Helps warn users about certain dangerous websites and downloads in supported Google products. Keep supported browser protection enabled.

Learn more

Google Play Protect

Built into supported Android devices through Google Play. It checks apps for potentially harmful behavior and provides security warnings.

Tip: Open the Play Store, select your profile, and look for Play Protect.

Official help

Bitwarden

A password manager with a free plan that can help users create, store, and organize unique passwords. Protect the account with a strong master password.

Official website

Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

CISA provides public cybersecurity guidance, awareness resources, and practical information about protecting accounts and devices.

Security guidance
Be careful with apps that promise perfect protection, request unnecessary permissions, or require you to disable your device's existing security features.
